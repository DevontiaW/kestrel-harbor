Read the factory

A resource’s capacity is its number of parallel stations × 60 ÷ minutes per bicycle. The smallest capacity sets the line’s ceiling. Adding capacity elsewhere can change waiting patterns without raising that ceiling.

Model assumptions and what the numbers mean

This is invented practice data from the existing Larkspur Cycle Works example, not a real factory or a forecast. The route is cutting/welding (45 min), painting (60), wheel building (30), then assembly (40). Parallel stations do not shorten one bicycle’s processing time.

Arrivals are evenly spaced, starting at minute 0. Work is first-in, first-out, with unlimited buffers. There are no failures, rejects, changeovers or travel delays. Painted floor paths show the route, but travel time is zero in the model. The 8-hour run starts empty; it needs 175 minutes before the first completion. Waiting and unfinished work remain at the end.

Capacity is a sustainable rate; finished bicycles is an observed simulation count. At the default release rate, the original line completes 6 bicycles in 8 hours; a second paint booth yields 8; adding a second assembly station as well yields 11. These differ from capacity × 8 because of startup. No steady-state Little’s Law claim is made for this transient or overloaded run. Queue drawings show up to 24 crates per stage; labels and totals include every unit.
