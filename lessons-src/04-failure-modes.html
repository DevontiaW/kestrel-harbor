What stops, and what keeps moving, when something fails?

The office internet drops for half an hour. People keep working on what is already on their desks. Nobody can send anything new out. When the connection returns, there is a burst of catching up, and the afternoon runs late.

A failure stops only the steps that depend on what failed. Everything else keeps working until the work in front of it runs out, and when the floor has spare capacity, the cost shows up as waiting rather than lost output.

On the agent floor, the research and planning agents call a model API (application programming interface): the remote service that answers their questions. Execution uses local tools. People do the review. Each depends on something different.

The town is generous in one way: a paused task resumes where it left off. Real outages can lose requests that were halfway done, which means repeated work on top of the delay.

Build it yourself with AI

Optional. This prompt adds a break-it switch to your simulator. It is a starter for learning, not a test plan for a business. Then check its work against the numbers below.

The one rule

A failure stops only the steps that depend on what failed. The time it costs is the minutes down times the tasks held there. Spare capacity afterwards decides whether that time also becomes lost output.

Starter prompt

Start from the single-file queue simulator with an event log that I built earlier (I will paste it below). Keep it one HTML file with no libraries. Add a break-it switch called "remote model service down":

- When it is on, it covers minutes {{build.l4.from}} to {{build.l4.to}} of the run.
- During those minutes, research and plan stop: tasks being worked there pause where they are, and tasks waiting in their lines cannot start. Execute and review keep going.
- Count every minute a task spends at research or plan during the outage, working or waiting, as "stopped by outage", separately from ordinary waiting.
- Draw those minutes in the timeline bar as dark stripes.
- Show totals: task-minutes stopped by the outage, tasks finished, and the average time in the system (all task-minutes divided by tasks that arrived).

What "done" looks like

  • A switch, and a run with it on and off.
  • Tasks caught in the outage show dark stripes on their bars.

Check the AI's work

  • 2 reviewers, outage on: {{build.l4.blocked}} task-minutes stopped by the outage.
  • Tasks finished change by {{cut.finishedDelta}}, and the average time in the system goes from {{cut.systemBase}} to {{cut.systemCut}} minutes.
  • Execute and review keep working during the outage. If they stop too, the AI has cut power, not the model service.
